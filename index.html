Why the conversion year matters

The strategy is to consider recognizing taxable conversion income during years when taxable income leaves room in relatively lower tax brackets.

Keep this simple

Direct rollover vs. paid to you.

Direct rollover
$10,000

Moves directly to the eligible retirement destination. Mandatory 20% rollover withholding generally does not apply — the full $10,000 can move to the destination.

Paid to you
$8,000

Certain eligible rollover distributions paid to the participant are generally subject to 20% mandatory federal withholding. To complete a full $10,000 rollover, the withheld amount generally must be replaced from other funds within the applicable rollover period.

Withholding is not the same thing as a tax or a penalty.
Getting this right

Rollover ≠ early cash withdrawal.

Income tax

Taxable conversion income

A pre‑tax → Roth conversion generally creates taxable income.

Withholding

A prepayment mechanism

Certain distributions paid to the participant can trigger mandatory 20% withholding.

10% additional tax

A separate, narrower rule

The 10% additional tax applies to certain early distributions — not to a properly structured conversion.

A properly executed eligible rollover or conversion should not be treated as an ordinary cash withdrawal simply because pre‑tax assets are moving to Roth. Later Roth distributions still have their own qualification and five‑year rules — Roth money is not automatically available penalty‑free the moment it converts.

In practice

How it can work.

01

Check the plan

Ask the 401(k) administrator whether the plan permits an in‑plan Roth rollover or an eligible direct rollover to a Roth IRA.

02

Choose the conversion amount

Consider taxable income and the tax brackets the conversion would affect.

03

Request a direct transaction

Request the applicable direct Roth rollover/conversion — not a distribution paid to your personal bank account.

04

Keep the retirement money invested

$10,000 Traditional → $10,000 Roth. The full amount continues to compound.

05

Handle the tax separately

The previously untaxed amount converted generally becomes taxable income. Use outside funds for the tax rather than reducing the amount remaining invested.

Plan rules vary. Confirm available options with the plan administrator before initiating a transaction.
